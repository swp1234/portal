    Frequently Asked Questions & Differential Science

    What is the key physiological difference between acute stress and burnout?

    Acute stress is characterized by over-engagement, sympathetic nervous system hyper-arousal (elevated adrenaline and cortisol), and emotional urgency. In contrast, burnout is characterized by disengagement, emotional blunting, chronic HPA-axis exhaustion, and cynical detachment. While stress feels like 'too much,' burnout feels like 'not enough left to give.'

    Can an individual suffer from acute stress and chronic burnout at the same time?

    Yes. This state is represented by the 'Crisis Overload' quadrant in our 2D differential matrix. It occurs when a person already depleted by chronic long-term burnout is confronted with an acute operational crisis, creating severe autonomic strain that requires immediate boundary-setting and decompression.

    Why does a typical weekend or short vacation fail to cure occupational burnout?

    Situational stress resolves relatively quickly once stressors are removed or managed. However, burnout involves structural neuro-endocrine dysregulation, altered dopamine signaling, and psychological exhaustion developed over months or years. Recovery requires systemic changes—workload renegotiation, boundary restoration, and weeks to months of autonomic baseline rebuilding.

    How is the 2D Differential score computed, and does this replace clinical evaluation?

    Scores are calculated by categorizing autonomic arousal markers (PSS-10 framework) against chronic exhaustion and detachment markers (Maslach Burnout Inventory framework). This assessment is an educational and self-reflection tool, not a clinical diagnostic instrument. If fatigue interferes with daily function, professional consultation is recommended.
