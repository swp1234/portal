Frequently Asked Questions & Somatic Science

How does the 5-4-3-2-1 technique stop panic attacks and acute anxiety?

The 5-4-3-2-1 method works through sensory grounding. During panic, the amygdala triggers a hyper-aroused sympathetic fight-or-flight state. Systematically directing conscious attention to external sensory cues (sight, touch, hearing, smell, taste) forces prefrontal cortex reactivation and inhibits internal catastrophic threat loops.

What is the physiological cyclic sigh and why is it included?

A physiological sigh consists of two consecutive nasal inhales followed by an extended, slow oral exhale. Research from Stanford University demonstrates that cyclic sighing is the fastest voluntary mechanism to lower autonomic heart rate and re-inflate collapsed pulmonary alveoli, increasing vagal tone within seconds.

Can this grounding technique assist during dissociation or trauma triggers?

Yes. Grounding techniques are widely used in Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) and trauma-informed care to orient individuals experiencing depersonalization or derealization back into the physical present.

Is my audio or usage data recorded during the exercise?

No. The entire exercise is 100% client-side. No microphones, cameras, or personal sensory notes are ever captured, transmitted, or logged.
