Drained by Dark Winter Days? The Morning Light Timing Routine That Lifts Mood

Struggling to wake up, craving heavy carbs, and feeling flat every afternoon once clocks fall back is not laziness—it is a delayed circadian signal starved of morning light.

Why Indoor Bulbs Cannot Wake Up Your Circadian Clock

Standard home lighting averages 200–400 lux, whereas outdoor daylight—even through gray clouds—delivers 2,000 to 10,000+ lux needed to shut off morning melatonin cleanly.

The 60-Minute Morning Light Window

Step outside with coffee or sit near a 10,000-lux bright-light lamp angled slightly off-center (never staring straight into LEDs) within your first hour awake.

Anchoring Afternoon Energy Before Sunset Drops

Pair a brisk 10-minute midday walk with warm low-glare evening lighting after 8 PM so your melatonin rhythm stays crisp all season.

FAQ

Does going outside on a cloudy winter day still help?

Yes! Overcast outdoor daylight is still dramatically brighter than indoor lamps and effectively triggers circadian alertness.

When is the best time to use bright light in winter?

Within the first 30 to 60 minutes after waking; late-evening bright light can delay bedtime instead.
