Why "Calm Down" Backfires Before a Speech or Interview

Anxiety and excitement share the exact same high-arousal heart rate and adrenaline surge. Trying to slam your nervous system from 100 to 0 feels unnatural; flipping the label from threat ("I’m nervous") to readiness ("I’m excited to share this") channels the energy smoothly.

The Illusion of Transparency: Audiences Notice Far Less Than You Think

Psychology experiments (Savitsky & Gilovich, 2003) show speakers consistently overestimate how visible their inner butterflies look. A brief 2-second pause sounds thoughtful and confident to listeners, never awkward.

Your 3-Minute Backstage Reset Checklist

1. Three physiological sighs (two quick nasal inhales, one long exhale) to soften vocal-cord tension.

2. Hum softly for 15 seconds to warm resonance.

3. Memorize ONLY your first opening sentence and deliver it 20% slower than normal.

FAQ

How do I stop my voice from shaking during a presentation?

Shaky voices come from shallow upper-chest breathing and throat tension. Doing 3 physiological sighs (long slow exhales), humming lightly for 15 seconds, and slowing your opening sentence immediately steadies vocal airflow.

Should I memorize a speech word-for-word to reduce anxiety?

No. Memorizing every word increases blank-mind panic. Memorize only your opening sentence and 3 signpost keywords so your speech stays natural.
