    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why is flying east usually harder for jet lag than flying west?

    Human circadian periods naturally run slightly longer than 24 hours (~24.2h), making it biologically easier to stay up later (flying west) than to force sleep hours earlier (flying east).

    Does meal timing really affect jet lag?

    Yes. Research by Wehrens et al. (2017) showed shifting meal times rapidly synchronizes peripheral metabolic clocks, reinforcing light cues to reduce daytime sluggishness.
