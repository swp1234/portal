    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why is anger often called a "secondary emotion" in psychology?

    The Anger Iceberg model shows that anger frequently sits above vulnerable primary feelings—like feeling disrespected, exhausted, unheard, or helpless—serving as fast protective energy to defend a boundary.

    Does venting aggressively (yelling or hitting pillows) actually reduce anger?

    Meta-analyses by Bushman and colleagues (2024) show aggressive arousal-increasing venting actually sustains physiological anger, whereas arousal-decreasing activities (slow breathing, cold water, brief pauses) rapidly cool the nervous system.
