Always 15 Minutes Late Even When You Care? Fix Time Blindness With Backward Buffers

Chronic lateness is rarely disrespect—it is usually the "planning fallacy" plus hidden transition minutes (keys, shoes, parking, elevator) that your brain deletes when guessing departure time.

Key Takeaway: Calculate departure backwards from 10 minutes before arrival, add a fixed 15-minute transition tax for shoes/keys/parking, and set a "stop everything" alarm 20 minutes before walking out.

Why Your Brain Always Estimates Best-Case Travel Time

Psychologists call this the planning fallacy: brains remember the one Sunday morning a drive took 18 minutes and ignore traffic, red lights, and walking from the parking lot.

The Invisible 15-Minute "Transition Tax"

Most people count commute time only—forgetting shutting down the laptop, finding keys, locking up, and waiting for elevators, which quietly consume 12 to 18 minutes.

Backward Chaining + The Hard Launch Alarm

Work backward from your arrival target minus 10 minutes, add your transition tax, and treat the resulting "launch alarm" as non-negotiable—never starting "one quick task" after it rings.

FAQ

Why am I always late even when I start getting ready early?

Starting early often creates a false sense of extra time, leading to "just one more small task" right before departure until the cushion vanishes.

How much buffer should I add for ADHD time blindness?

Add 15 minutes for home-exit transitions plus 25% of your expected transit duration so normal friction never makes you late.
