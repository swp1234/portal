    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is "anchor sleep" for night-shift workers?

    Anchor sleep (Minors & Waterhouse; Eastman) means keeping a consistent 3-to-4-hour block of sleep at the same clock hours across both work days and days off so your circadian rhythm stabilizes rather than constantly jet-lagging.

    Why do night-shift workers wear dark sunglasses driving home?

    Morning blue daylight suppresses melatonin rapidly and signals daytime wakefulness to the brain’s circadian pacemaker—blocking that light during the commute makes falling asleep at home dramatically easier.
