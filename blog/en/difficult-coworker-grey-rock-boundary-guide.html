Long defensive explanations feed high-conflict coworkers. Responding like a courteous, boring pebble—warm-neutral, factual, and 2 sentences max—keeps your reputation spotless and your energy yours.

Why Defensive Over-Explaining Backfires With High-Conflict Peers

Every extra paragraph gives a baiting colleague new phrasing to twist. Two courteous factual sentences close the loop cleanly.

The 4-Word BIFF Formula (Brief / Informative / Friendly / Firm)

"Thanks for flagging—I’m sticking to the agreed sprint plan and will share the doc at 3 PM." Polite tone, zero emotional fuel.

Move Verbal Ambiguity Into Calm Written Summaries

A friendly "Just recapping our alignment in writing so nothing slips" prevents credit-stealing or memory rewrites without sounding accusatory.

FAQ

Will using Grey Rock at work make me seem cold or uncooperative?

Done professionally, you stay warm-neutral and reliable on actual tasks while simply declining emotional bait, gossip, and personal oversharing.

How should I respond to a passive-aggressive email or Slack message?

Wait 10 minutes, ignore the snarky tone completely, answer only the factual business question in 1–2 polite lines, and move on.
