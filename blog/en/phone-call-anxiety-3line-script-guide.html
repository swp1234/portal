Putting Off a 2-Minute Phone Call for Weeks? Beat Call Anxiety With a 3-Line Sticky Note

Real-time voice calls remove facial cues and editing time—triggering fear of blanking out. Putting your opening line and key ID on paper removes 90% of the dread.

Key Takeaway: Write 3 bullets on a sticky note (1-line opener, account/date details, callback number for voicemail), stand up to open your breath, count 3-2-1, and tap Call.

Why Two Minutes of Call Dread Costs Two Weeks of Guilt

Avoidance feels relieving for 5 seconds, yet carrying an open loop on your to-do list drains mental energy every single day.

The 3-Line Sticky Note That Prevents Mind-Blanks

Read line 1 aloud ("Hi, I’m [Name], calling to check [X]"), keep a friendly buffer ready ("Give me two seconds to grab that"), and keep a pen in hand.

Stand Up & Batch Two Routine Calls Back-to-Back

Standing steadies vocal tone and diaphragm airflow; once Call #1 ends in relief, momentum glides you through Call #2.

FAQ

Why do phone calls trigger anxiety even when texting feels effortless?

Phone calls demand instant unedited responses without visual body-language cues, activating anticipatory social-evaluation fear.

What should I say if I forget a detail mid-call?

A casual "Give me just two seconds to pull that up!" sounds completely normal and buys all the calm breathing room you need.
