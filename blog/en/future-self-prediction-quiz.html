FUTURE SELF · FIXED-RULE STORY

What Will You Be Like in 10 Years?

Choose eight scenes from an imagined future day. The story returns the theme favored by this set of choices, then helps you test one related action for seven days.

Read this first: the activity does not predict your job, success, personality, finances, relationships, health, or life in 10 years. It is a recreational snapshot of eight current choices—not an AI forecast, psychological assessment, or professional advice.

HOW THE RESULT IS MADE

A published fixed rule, not a forecast

1

Choose eight scenes

Each scene asks you to pick one of three ways an imagined future day could unfold.

2

Add two fixed points

Every answer adds one point to two of eight story themes. There is no model call, confidence score, or hidden weighting.

3

Show the highest theme

The highest total wins. Ties use one published order: CEO, artist, adventurer, scholar, healer, influencer, inventor, freelancer.

8 choices × 2 theme points = 16 assigned points → one highest-scoring story label

The labels are fictional story shorthand. “CEO” does not predict an occupation; “healer” does not describe a clinical role. A different mood or context can produce different choices.

EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

Imagining a future self is not predicting one

Future-self experiments

Hershfield and colleagues tested age-progressed self images in specific virtual-reality and decision tasks. Some studies found more future-oriented saving choices after that exposure.

If–then planning

Research on implementation intentions examines plans that connect a defined situation with a defined response. It supports testing a concrete action rather than treating a broad identity wish as completed.

What neither source proves

Those studies do not validate DopaBrain's eight labels, predict an individual's future, or guarantee that this story changes behavior. The app is a transparent reflection activity.

SEVEN-DAY REALITY CHECK

Replace the label with one observable action

  1. Pick one sentence in the result that feels relevant now; ignore the rest.
  2. Write one if–then action: “If it is 7 p.m. on Tuesday, then I will spend 10 minutes sketching,” for example.
  3. After seven days, judge the action by what happened—not by whether the story label felt flattering.

Sources and limits

  • Hershfield et al., Increasing Saving Behavior Through Age-Progressed Renderings of the Future Self
  • Gollwitzer & Sheeran, Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement

The first source concerns specific future-self and saving tasks; the second concerns if–then planning across research settings. Neither is evidence that this DopaBrain story predicts a career, outcome, or personality.
