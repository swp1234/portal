Doomscrolling: what it is and a 60-second stop plan

Doomscrolling is continuing through distressing news or posts even after the session stops feeling useful. You do not need a brain label or a perfect detox to interrupt the next loop.

Health boundary: This guide is general education, not a diagnosis or treatment. Research below reports associations, not proof that scrolling causes a specific mental-health outcome.

WHAT TO NOTICE

A loop is more useful than a label

There is no clear question

You opened a feed without deciding what information you needed or when you would stop.

The next post feels urgent

Refreshing promises resolution, but each item creates another reason to continue.

The session displaces something

Sleep, a task, movement, or a conversation keeps getting pushed back.

A 2023 study that developed a doomscrolling scale found associations with psychological distress and lower well-being. It did not establish a universal cutoff or prove causation. A later cross-cultural study also reported an association with existential anxiety in convenience samples from Iran and the United States.

MAKE THE EXIT EASIER

Three changes that do not require willpower alone

1. Define the stop

Before opening a feed, name the question and set a short timer. When it rings, close the feed before deciding whether you need more.

2. Add friction

Move the app off the home screen, mute nonessential notifications, or use your device's app timer or focus mode.

3. Prepare a replacement

Put one small alternative within reach: water, a short walk, a saved article, music, or a message to someone you trust.

The CDC includes taking breaks from news and social media among general stress-management suggestions. Android's official Digital Wellbeing controls can show app use, set app or site timers, and pause distracting apps. These are options, not guaranteed treatments.
