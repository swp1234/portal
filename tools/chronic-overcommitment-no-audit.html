    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the "Yes-Damn Effect" in psychology?

    Discovered by Zauberman and Lynch (2005), people systematically expect to have far more spare time in the future than today—causing them to say "yes" to future commitments they regret ("damn!") once the date arrives.

    Why should I avoid giving long excuses when saying no?

    Long explanations accidentally invite others to problem-solve around your excuse. A warm, appreciative, two-sentence boundary ("Thanks for thinking of me—I don’t have bandwidth this month") is clearer and kinder to everyone.

    Psychological Frameworks & References
